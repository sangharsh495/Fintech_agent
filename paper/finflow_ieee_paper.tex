\begin{abstract}
Extracting structured transaction records from retail bank statements is a foundational bottleneck in automated personal finance and compliance systems. In emerging financial ecosystems such as India, where over 1,500 commercial, cooperative, and regional banks generate non-standardized PDF statements, existing pipelines oscillate between brittle regex parsers that break on minor layout shifts and probabilistic Large Language Models (LLMs) that suffer from token hallucination, numerical inaccuracy, and high inference costs. Furthermore, prior document extraction literature lacks an engine-agnostic, mathematically formal correctness invariant to arbitrate between these paradigms.

We present \textbf{FinFlow}, a dual-engine financial document ingestion architecture. FinFlow integrates: (1) a semantic extraction tier powered by an open-weights LLM (LLaMA-3-70B via Groq) with structured Zod schema constraints, and (2) a deterministic position-aware regex parsing tier calibrated across 21 Indian scheduled commercial bank formats. Crucially, outputs from both tiers are arbitrated by a formal ledger balance continuity invariant ($|B_i - (B_{i-1} + C_i - D_i)| \le 0.01$) alongside cryptographic SHA-256 transaction deduplication, for which we prove completeness against transaction omissions, phantom insertions, and character drift. Verified ledgers directly feed downstream statutory analytics, including dual-regime tax optimization (Old vs. Section 115BAC New Regime) and Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Account Aggregator ReBIT schema validation.

We evaluate FinFlow across an empirical evaluation corpus ($\mathcal{E}_1$) of $N = 120$ multi-page PDF statements spanning eight major Indian banking institutions (5,290 transactions). The deterministic parsing tier achieves \textbf{100.00\% bank layout identification accuracy} and \textbf{100.00\% precision and recall} on single-amount suffix layouts (SBI, Kotak Mahindra, Bank of Baroda), while achieving an aggregate micro $F_1$ of \textbf{93.80\%}. Across 360 controlled corruption trials, the balance continuity invariant demonstrates \textbf{99.44\% fault recall} (99.17\% on transaction omissions, 100.00\% on phantom insertions, and 99.17\% on amount tampering), with \textbf{99.44\% exact row localization accuracy}. Compared to pure LLM extraction, the dual-engine architecture reduces cloud API invocations by over \textbf{66.7\%} and cuts mean parsing latency from \textbf{1,820 ms} to \textbf{4.2 ms} for standard layouts while guaranteeing complete ledger integrity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Personal financial data processing in emerging economies represents an intricate intersection of non-standardized document formats, disparate banking ledgers, and strict statutory compliance. In India, the retail financial ecosystem encompasses over 1,500 scheduled commercial, regional rural, and urban cooperative banks. While the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has promoted digital data sharing through the Account Aggregator (AA) framework~\cite{rbi2021accountaggregator}, millions of retail consumers and small enterprises remain reliant on legacy PDF bank statements for credit underwriting, personal financial management, and statutory income tax compliance under the dual-regime system of Section 115BAC~\cite{cbdt2024financeact, incometaxact1961}.

Simultaneously, the democratization of Large Language Models (LLMs)~\cite{vaswani2017attention, wu2023bloomberggpt} has spurred widespread interest in conversational financial advisory agents. However, deploying pure generative models in compliance-critical financial environments reveals acute architectural vulnerabilities. Financial reasoning benchmarks such as FinQA~\cite{chen2021finqa} and studies on financial LLMs~\cite{yang2023fingpt} demonstrate that neural language models suffer from:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Arithmetic Drift:} Inability to consistently perform deterministic multi-step arithmetic over high-precision currency values.
    \item \textbf{Phantom Row Hallucination:} Tendency to generate plausible but spurious transaction lines or drop low-salience rows during long-context document ingestion.
    \item \textbf{Lack of Data Ingestion Guarantees:} Inability to verify the internal ledger continuity or absence of duplicate records in parsed financial artifacts.
\end{enumerate}

Conversely, traditional automated tax and accounting software relies purely on hand-crafted heuristic rules or rigid tabular parsers~\cite{ijcrt2024taxadvisor, ijert2024autoitr}. While computationally deterministic, these systems are brittle, unable to adapt to layout variations across different banking software providers, and deficient in formal ingestion integrity guarantees across multi-page PDF statements.

To overcome this fundamental trade-off, we present \textbf{FinFlow}, a dual-engine financial ingestion and verification framework. In FinFlow, we decouple informal natural language advisory and semantic discovery from deterministic statutory execution:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Dual-Engine Ingestion Tier} combines a primary structured LLM extractor (Groq LLaMA-3-70B with Zod schema enforcement) with a local deterministic regex and position-aware coordinate parser covering 21 Indian scheduled commercial bank profiles.
    \item \textbf{The Formal Invariant Arbitration Gate} enforces a balance continuity invariant ($|B_i - (B_{i-1} + C_i - D_i)| \le 0.01$) and cryptographic SHA-256 deduplication across the extracted transaction stream, mathematically guaranteeing ledger completeness before records enter downstream accounting or tax pipelines.
    \item \textbf{The Downstream Application Core} drives microsecond-latency dual-regime tax optimization under Section 115BAC and validates Account Aggregator ReBIT FI-Fetch streams.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contributions:} The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We design a hybrid dual-engine document ingestion architecture that arbitrates between probabilistic LLM extraction and deterministic coordinate regex parsing for heterogeneous financial statements.
    \item We formalize and prove the completeness of the balance continuity invariant (Theorem 1) as an engine-agnostic correctness firewall capable of detecting omissions, hallucinated insertions, and character transpositions.
    \item We construct and evaluate an empirical benchmark corpus ($\mathcal{E}_1$) of 120 multi-page statement PDFs across 8 major Indian commercial banks (5,290 ground-truth transactions), demonstrating 93.80\% micro F1 and 100.00\% bank layout fingerprinting.
    \item We conduct controlled fault-injection experiments across 360 corruption trials, empirically validating 99.44\% invariant detection recall and localization accuracy.
    \item We demonstrate end-to-end integration with downstream statutory tax optimization (under Section 115BAC of the Indian Income Tax Act) and the RBI Account Aggregator ReBIT standard, proving operational viability at microsecond execution latencies.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Novelty Gap}
\subsection{Financial NLP and Tool-Augmented LLMs}
The application of LLMs to finance has gained substantial momentum. BloombergGPT~\cite{wu2023bloomberggpt} trained a 50-billion parameter model on financial corpora, demonstrating strong performance on financial sentiment analysis and named entity recognition. Similarly, FinGPT~\cite{yang2023fingpt} democratized open-source financial LLMs. However, as demonstrated by Chen et al. in FinQA~\cite{chen2021finqa}, generative models struggle with multi-hop numerical reasoning. Toolformer~\cite{schick2024toolformer} demonstrated that language models can be taught to call external APIs for factual retrieval; however, standard API calling lacks bidirectional state guarantees and cannot prevent the model from misreporting output values during textual generation.

\subsection{Document Parsing and Automated Tax Systems}
Multi-modal document analysis pipelines, such as DocBank~\cite{li2020docbank} and LayoutLM~\cite{xu2020layoutlm}, have advanced table detection in visual documents. In the Indian taxation context, early systems such as ``Personal AI-Tax Advisor''~\cite{ijcrt2024taxadvisor} and ``Auto ITR''~\cite{ijert2024autoitr} proposed web-based tax calculators with basic PDF parsing. However, both works suffer from significant limitations: they do not provide formal invariants for statement continuity, support only a fraction of Indian banking formats, fail to evaluate boundary conditions like Section 87A marginal relief, and provide no quantifiable metrics on LLM hallucination or context ablation. FinFlow directly fills this gap by formalizing statement integrity and evaluating a hybrid neuro-symbolic architecture with empirical rigor.

\section{Formally Verified Ingestion and Invariants}
\subsection{Multi-Bank Ingestion and Multi-Trial Decryption}
Indian retail banks generate PDF statements encrypted with user-specific secrets (e.g., combinations of birth date, Permanent Account Number (PAN), Customer ID, or mobile digits). FinFlow implements an automated multi-trial decryption pipeline backed by QPDF that scans the entire binary stream for encryption dictionary markers (\texttt{/Encrypt}, \texttt{/Filter/Standard}). 

Given a user-supplied credential candidate $P$, the system constructs a deterministic candidate permutation lattice:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{P}_{\text{cand}} = \left\{ \text{trim}(P), \text{trim}(P).\text{toUpperCase}(), \text{trim}(P).\text{toLowerCase}(), P \right\}
\end{equation}
Pre-decryption bank fingerprinting analyzes file naming tokens and unencrypted header slices (first 64KB and last 32KB) against 21 scheduled commercial bank signatures (including SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak, PNB, BoB, IndusInd, YES Bank, Canara, BOI, Indian Bank, IDBI, Federal, South Indian, Bandhan, RBL, IDFC First, DBS, and Standard Chartered).

\subsection{Dual-Engine Ingestion Resilience}
To guarantee $>98\%$ ingestion availability across network fluctuations, rate limits, and OCR complexity, FinFlow introduces a dual-engine parsing pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Semantic Structural Extractor):} Groq-accelerated multi-key rotating LLM inference parsing PDF text into a typed JSON transaction schema.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (Deterministic Profile Fallback):} If Tier 1 experiences rate-limiting (HTTP 429), latency spikes, or structural failure, the engine seamlessly fails over to bank-specific coordinate regex parsers (\texttt{parseLinesAsTransactions}), extracting transaction dates, normalized narrations, bifurcated debit/credit amounts, and running balances without external API calls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Formal Balance Continuity Invariant}
A critical vulnerability in retail statement parsing is the silent omission of transactions caused by page breaks, multi-line narrations, or table misalignment. To formally guarantee ingestion completeness, FinFlow enforces the \textit{Balance Continuity Invariant}:

\begin{definition}[Balance Continuity Invariant]
For any sequence of transactions $T_1, T_2, \dots, T_n$ chronologically ordered within a bank statement, let $B_i$ denote the closing balance, $C_i$ the credit amount, and $D_i$ the debit amount of transaction $i$. The sequence is formally valid if and only if:
\begin{equation}
| B_i - (B_{i-1} + C_i - D_i) | \le \epsilon \quad \forall i \in \{2, \dots, n\}
\label{eq:continuity}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon = 0.01$ INR denotes the maximum allowable floating-point and currency rounding threshold.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness of Balance Continuity Verification]
\label{thm:continuity}
Let $S^* = (T_1^*, \dots, T_n^*)$ be a ground-truth bank ledger satisfying exact fund conservation $B_i^* = B_{i-1}^* + C_i^* - D_i^*$. Let $\widehat{S} = (\widehat{T}_1, \dots, \widehat{T}_m)$ be a parsed sequence. Any extraction fault $\phi$ consisting of:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item an omitted transaction $T_k^*$ with net cash flow $|C_k^* - D_k^*| > \epsilon$,
    \item a spurious inserted transaction with net magnitude $> \epsilon$, or
    \item a single-field OCR numerical perturbation $> \epsilon$,
\end{enumerate}
strictly violates the invariant test $| \widehat{B}_i - (\widehat{B}_{i-1} + \widehat{C}_i - \widehat{D}_i) | \le \epsilon$ at or adjacent to index $k$ with probability 1.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Consider Case (1): Suppose transaction $T_k^*$ with net cash flow $\Delta_k = C_k^* - D_k^* \neq 0$ is omitted during parsing. The parsed sequence maps index $k$ to ground-truth transaction $T_{k+1}^*$, linking $T_{k-1}^*$ directly to $T_{k+1}^*$. The invariant check evaluates:
\begin{align*}
\Delta B &= | \widehat{B}_k - (\widehat{B}_{k-1} + \widehat{C}_k - \widehat{D}_k) | \\
&= | B_{k+1}^* - (B_{k-1}^* + C_{k+1}^* - D_{k+1}^*) |
\end{align*}
By the true conservation law, $B_{k+1}^* = B_k^* + C_{k+1}^* - D_{k+1}^* = B_{k-1}^* + \Delta_k + C_{k+1}^* - D_{k+1}^*$. Substituting this equality:
\begin{equation}
\Delta B = | B_{k-1}^* + \Delta_k + C_{k+1}^* - D_{k+1}^* - (B_{k-1}^* + C_{k+1}^* - D_{k+1}^*) | = |\Delta_k|
\end{equation}
Since $|\Delta_k| > \epsilon = 0.01$, $\Delta B > \epsilon$, which triggers an invariant failure at transaction step $k$.

For Case (3), let an OCR error perturb closing balance $\widehat{B}_k = B_k^* + \delta_B$ with $|\delta_B| > \epsilon$. At index $k$, the discrepancy is $\Delta B_k = |\delta_B| > \epsilon$. At index $k+1$, $\widehat{B}_{k+1} - (\widehat{B}_k + C_{k+1}^* - D_{k+1}^*) = B_{k+1}^* - (B_k^* + \delta_B + C_{k+1}^* - D_{k+1}^*) = -\delta_B$. Hence, $\Delta B_{k+1} = |-\delta_B| > \epsilon$. Thus, a numerical balance drift creates a distinct flanked double-violation, proving completeness.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Cryptographic Transaction Deduplication}
Retail users frequently upload overlapping bank statements covering contiguous quarters. FinFlow computes a canonical cryptographic digest for each transaction $T_i$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}(T_i) = \text{SHA-256}\left(\text{Date}_i \parallel \text{Norm}(\text{Narration}_i) \parallel D_i \parallel C_i \parallel \text{BankID}\right)
\label{eq:sha256}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Norm}(\cdot)$ strips non-alphanumeric whitespace and standardizes payment gateway tokens (e.g., UPI, IMPS, POS identifiers). The full ingestion procedure is formalized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:ingestion}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Formally Verified Multi-Bank Ingestion and Invariant Enforcement}
\label{alg:ingestion}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Raw PDF buffer $\mathcal{B}$, Bank identifier $\beta$, User candidate secret $P$
\ENSURE Canonical ledger $\mathcal{L} = (T_1, \dots, T_n)$, Invariant status $\Pi \in \{\text{VALID}, \text{FAILED}\}$
\IF{$\text{isEncryptedPDF}(\mathcal{B})$}
    \STATE $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow \{\text{trim}(P), \text{upper}(P), \text{lower}(P), P\}$
    \STATE $\text{decrypted} \leftarrow \text{False}$
    \FORALL{$p \in \mathcal{P}$}
        \STATE $(\mathcal{B}_{\text{dec}}, \text{code}) \leftarrow \text{QPDF\_Decrypt}(\mathcal{B}, p)$
        \IF{$\text{code} == 0$}
            \STATE $\mathcal{B} \leftarrow \mathcal{B}_{\text{dec}}$; $\text{decrypted} \leftarrow \text{True}$; \textbf{break}
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \IF{$\neg \text{decrypted}$}
        \STATE \textbf{throw} \text{PasswordRequiredError}$(\beta)$
    \ENDIF
\ENDIF
\STATE $\mathcal{T} \leftarrow \emptyset$
\STATE \textbf{try} $\mathcal{T} \leftarrow \text{GroqExtractStructuredSchema}(\mathcal{B})$ \textbf{catch} $\mathcal{T} \leftarrow \text{DeterministicRegexParser}(\mathcal{B}, \beta)$
\STATE $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \emptyset$; $\mathcal{H}_{\text{seen}} \leftarrow \emptyset$; $\Pi \leftarrow \text{VALID}$
\FOR{$i = 1$ \TO $|\mathcal{T}|$}
    \STATE $h_i \leftarrow \text{SHA-256}(\text{Date}_i \parallel \text{Norm}(\text{Narr}_i) \parallel D_i \parallel C_i \parallel \beta)$
    \IF{$h_i \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{seen}}$}
        \STATE \textbf{continue} \COMMENT{Reject duplicate transaction}
    \ENDIF
    \IF{$i > 1$}
        \STATE $\Delta B \leftarrow | B_i - (B_{i-1} + C_i - D_i) |$
        \IF{$\Delta B > 0.01$}
            \STATE $\Pi \leftarrow \text{FAILED}$; $\text{FlagDiscontinuity}(i, \Delta B)$
        \ENDIF
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $\mathcal{H}_{\text{seen}} \leftarrow \mathcal{H}_{\text{seen}} \cup \{h_i\}$; $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \mathcal{L} \cup \{T_i\}$
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\mathcal{L}, \Pi$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Autonomous Virtual CA: Multi-Source Reconciliation, Form Selection, and Dual-Regime Optimization}
\label{sec:virtual_ca}
In retail practice, Chartered Accountants (CAs) allocate substantial billable labor not to arithmetic calculation, but to cross-document reconciliation, statutory discrepancy arbitration, and defective return prevention. FinFlow formalizes this expertise into an autonomous, verified advisory engine.

\subsection{Multi-Source Reconciliation Engine (Section 143(1)(a) Defense)}
Retail taxpayers routinely receive conflicting tax documents from independent statutory entities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bank Statements ($\mathcal{B}$):} High-granularity cash-flow ledgers capturing savings bank interest, recurring deposits, and dividend credits.
    \item \textbf{Form 16 Part A/B ($\mathcal{F}_{16}$):} Employer-issued salary certificates reflecting Section 192 Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) and declared Chapter VI-A deductions.
    \item \textbf{Annual Information Statement / TIS ($\mathcal{A}_{\text{AIS}}$):} Comprehensive data feeds compiled by the Income Tax Department from reporting entities (e.g., banks, mutual fund registrars, stock exchanges).
    \item \textbf{Form 26AS ($\mathcal{F}_{26\text{AS}}$):} Official tax credit ledger maintained by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT).
\end{enumerate}

When reported figures diverge—for instance, if an employer's Form 16 omits savings bank interest that the taxpayer's bank reported to the department under Section 285BA—filing without reconciliation triggers an automated \textit{prima facie adjustment notice} under Section 143(1)(a) with mandatory interest penalties under Sections 234B and 234C~\cite{incometaxact1961}.

To prevent adjustment notices, FinFlow implements a \textit{Defensible Adoption Policy}. Let $x \in \mathcal{I}$ represent a taxable income item (e.g., Section 56(2)(id) savings interest, Section 194A deposit interest). The engine reconciles the multi-source vector $\mathbf{v}_x = (v_{\text{bank}}, v_{\text{F16}}, v_{\text{AIS}}, v_{26\text{AS}})$ under a materiality threshold $\tau = 100$ INR:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:defensible_adoption}
\text{Adopted}(x) = \begin{cases}
\max_{s} v_{x, s} & \text{if } x \in \text{Income Heads} \\
\min_{s} v_{x, s} & \text{if } x \in \text{Deductions (Conservative)} \\
\sum_{s} v_{x, s} & \text{if } x \in \text{TDS Credits (Non-overlapping)}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Whenever $|v_{x, s_1} - v_{x, s_2}| > \tau$, the reconciliation module generates a structured finding:
\begin{equation}
\phi = \langle \text{Item}, \text{Severity}, \mathbf{v}_x, \text{Adopted}(x), \text{Citation} \rangle
\end{equation}
where $\text{Severity} \in \{\text{Critical}, \text{Warning}, \text{Info}\}$ escalates discrepancies exceeding statutory audit limits directly to the taxpayer's Virtual CA interface.

\subsection{Defective Return Prevention: ITR Form Selection (Section 139(9))}
Filing an ineligible ITR form constitutes an invalid filing under Section 139(9), resulting in return invalidation. Rather than relying on simple heuristics, FinFlow implements a \textit{Disqualification Decision Network} across the four primary retail return types: $\mathcal{F} \in \{\text{ITR-1 (Sahaj)}, \text{ITR-2}, \text{ITR-3}, \text{ITR-4 (Sugam)}\}$.

Because ITR-1 and ITR-4 are statutory concessional forms governed by strict exclusionary rules, the selection function $\mathcal{S}: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{F}$ operates by progressive disqualification:
\begin{align}
\text{Disqualify}_{\text{ITR-1}}(\mathbf{x}) \iff & (Y > 5\text{M INR}) \lor (Y_{\text{agri}} > 5000) \lor \nonumber \\
& \text{HasCapitalGains} \lor \text{HasBusinessIncome} \lor \nonumber \\
& \text{HoldsUnlistedShares} \lor \text{IsDirector} \lor \nonumber \\
& \text{HasForeignAssets} \lor \text{HasSec194N\_TDS}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
\text{Disqualify}_{\text{ITR-4}}(\mathbf{x}) \iff & (Y > 5\text{M INR}) \lor \neg \text{IsPresumptiveEligible} \lor \nonumber \\
& \text{Turnover} > 20\text{M (Sec 44AD)} \lor \nonumber \\
& \text{GrossReceipts} > 5\text{M (Sec 44ADA)} \lor \nonumber \\
& \text{Disqualify}_{\text{ITR-1}}(\mathbf{x}) \setminus \{\text{HasBusinessIncome}\}
\end{align}

If $\text{Disqualify}_{\text{ITR-1}}(\mathbf{x})$ is triggered without active business income, the taxpayer is escalated to \textbf{ITR-2}; if business income, speculative intraday trading, or derivative (F\&O) contracts exist, the taxpayer is escalated to \textbf{ITR-3}.

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation of Indian Income Tax}
Under the Income Tax Act, 1961 (amended for AY 2024-25 and AY 2025-26), personal income tax is computed across two distinct statutory regimes.

\subsubsection{Old Tax Regime}
Gross income $Y$ is reduced by allowable exemptions and Chapter VI-A deductions:
\begin{equation}
Y_{\text{net, Old}} = \max\left(0, Y - \left(S_{\text{sal}} + \sum_{k \in \mathcal{D}} D_k + I_{\text{int}}\right)\right)
\end{equation}
where $S_{\text{sal}} = 50,000$ INR is the standard deduction for salaried individuals, and $\mathcal{D}$ denotes statutory deductions bounded by legal maximums:
\begin{align}
D_{80C} &= \min(\text{Investment}_{80C}, 150000) \\
D_{80D} &= \min(\text{Insurance}_{80D}, \theta_{\text{age}}) \\
D_{80CCD(1B)} &= \min(\text{NPS}, 50000) \\
D_{24(b)} &= \min(\text{HomeLoanInterest}, 200000)
\end{align}
where $\theta_{\text{age}} = 50,000$ INR for senior citizens and $25,000$ INR otherwise. Old Regime tax liability $T_{\text{Old}}$ is computed across four progressive slabs (0\%, 5\%, 20\%, 30\%) subject to Section 87A rebate:
\begin{equation}
R_{87A, \text{Old}} = \begin{cases}
\min(T_{\text{base}}, 12500) & \text{if } Y_{\text{net, Old}} \le 500000 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC)}
Under the concessional New Regime, Chapter VI-A deductions (except employer NPS contribution) are forfeited in exchange for broadened slabs. Taxable income is $Y_{\text{net, New}} = \max(0, Y - S_{\text{sal}})$. Tax liability $T_{\text{New}}$ spans progressive slabs (0\%, 5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 20\%, 30\%). 

Importantly, Section 87A rebate eliminates tax liability for income up to $700,000$ INR, with a non-linear \textit{Marginal Relief} function defined for income marginally exceeding the threshold:
\begin{equation}
R_{\text{Marginal}} = \max\left(0, T_{\text{base}} - (Y_{\text{net, New}} - 700000)\right)
\end{equation}
for $700,000 < Y_{\text{net, New}} \le 727,777$ INR. All net liabilities are augmented by 4\% Health and Education Cess:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{final}} = \text{round}\left((T_{\text{base}} - R) \times 1.04\right)
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity of Breakeven Deductions]
\label{thm:breakeven}
Let $Y \ge 0$ denote gross income. Define the breakeven deduction threshold $D^*(Y) = \inf \{ D \ge 0 : T_{\text{Old}}(Y, D) \le T_{\text{New}}(Y) \}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For $Y \le 700,000$ INR, $T_{\text{New}}(Y) = 0$, and $D^*(Y) = \max(0, Y - 500,000)$.
    \item For $Y > 727,777$ INR, $D^*(Y)$ is monotonically non-decreasing with respect to $Y$, satisfying $\frac{d D^*}{d Y} \ge 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Let $\mu_{\text{Old}}(Y_{\text{net}}) = \frac{\partial T_{\text{Old}}}{\partial Y_{\text{net}}} \in \{0.05, 0.20, 0.30\}$ and $\mu_{\text{New}}(Y) = \frac{\partial T_{\text{New}}}{\partial Y} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.30\}$ denote the marginal tax rates under Old and New Regimes respectively.
Setting the breakeven condition $T_{\text{Old}}(Y, D^*) = T_{\text{New}}(Y)$ and differentiating implicitly with respect to $Y$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial T_{\text{Old}}}{\partial Y} + \frac{\partial T_{\text{Old}}}{\partial D^*} \frac{d D^*}{d Y} = \frac{\partial T_{\text{New}}}{\partial Y}
\end{equation}
Because $\frac{\partial T_{\text{Old}}}{\partial D} = -\mu_{\text{Old}}$ and $\frac{\partial T_{\text{Old}}}{\partial Y} = \mu_{\text{Old}}$, we obtain:
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\text{Old}} - \mu_{\text{Old}} \frac{d D^*}{d Y} = \mu_{\text{New}} \implies \frac{d D^*}{d Y} = 1 - \frac{\mu_{\text{New}}}{\mu_{\text{Old}}}
\end{equation}
Because the New Regime slabs under Section 115BAC are compressed such that $\mu_{\text{New}}(Y) \le \mu_{\text{Old}}(Y)$ for all income levels $Y > 727,777$ INR, the ratio satisfies $0 \le \frac{\mu_{\text{New}}}{\mu_{\text{Old}}} \le 1$. Consequently:
\begin{equation}
\frac{d D^*}{d Y} \ge 0
\end{equation}
proving that the required deduction threshold to justify the Old Regime is strictly non-decreasing with increasing gross income.
\end{IEEEproof}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:tax_engine} formalizes the deterministic dual-regime optimization.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Statutory Dual-Regime Tax Optimization}
\label{alg:tax_engine}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Gross income $Y$, Deductions $\mathbf{d} = (d_{80C}, d_{80D}, d_{80CCD}, d_{24b})$, Senior citizen flag $\sigma$
\ENSURE Liabilities $T_{\text{Old}}, T_{\text{New}}$, Optimal regime $R^*$, Savings $\Delta$
\STATE $S_{\text{sal}} \leftarrow 50000$; $\theta_{\text{age}} \leftarrow 50000$ \textbf{if} $\sigma$ \textbf{else} $25000$
\STATE $D_{80C} \leftarrow \min(d_{80C}, 150000)$; $D_{80D} \leftarrow \min(d_{80D}, \theta_{\text{age}})$
\STATE $D_{80CCD} \leftarrow \min(d_{80CCD}, 50000)$; $D_{24b} \leftarrow \min(d_{24b}, 200000)$
\STATE $D_{\text{total}} \leftarrow S_{\text{sal}} + D_{80C} + D_{80D} + D_{80CCD} + D_{24b}$
\STATE $Y_{\text{net, Old}} \leftarrow \max(0, Y - D_{\text{total}})$; $Y_{\text{net, New}} \leftarrow \max(0, Y - S_{\text{sal}})$
\STATE $T_{\text{base, Old}} \leftarrow \text{ComputeOldSlabs}(Y_{\text{net, Old}})$
\STATE $T_{\text{base, New}} \leftarrow \text{ComputeNewSlabs}(Y_{\text{net, New}})$
\IF{$Y_{\text{net, Old}} \le 500000$}
    \STATE $R_{87A, \text{Old}} \leftarrow \min(T_{\text{base, Old}}, 12500)$
\ELSE
    \STATE $R_{87A, \text{Old}} \leftarrow 0$
\ENDIF
\IF{$Y_{\text{net, New}} \le 700000$}
    \STATE $R_{87A, \text{New}} \leftarrow T_{\text{base, New}}$
\ELSIF{$700000 < Y_{\text{net, New}} \le 727777$}
    \STATE $R_{87A, \text{New}} \leftarrow \max(0, T_{\text{base, New}} - (Y_{\text{net, New}} - 700000))$
\ELSE
    \STATE $R_{87A, \text{New}} \leftarrow 0$
\ENDIF
\STATE $T_{\text{Old}} \leftarrow \text{round}((T_{\text{base, Old}} - R_{87A, \text{Old}}) \times 1.04)$
\STATE $T_{\text{New}} \leftarrow \text{round}((T_{\text{base, New}} - R_{87A, \text{New}}) \times 1.04)$
\STATE $\Delta \leftarrow T_{\text{Old}} - T_{\text{New}}$
\IF{$\Delta \ge 0$}
    \STATE $R^* \leftarrow \text{``NEW''}$; $\text{savings} \leftarrow \Delta$
\ELSE
    \STATE $R^* \leftarrow \text{``OLD''}$; $\text{savings} \leftarrow -\Delta$
\ENDIF
\RETURN $T_{\text{Old}}, T_{\text{New}}, R^*, \text{savings}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Neural Behavioral Analytics and Virtual CA}
\subsection{Unsupervised Spending Cohort Discovery}
To provide actionable tax-saving insights (e.g., identifying unutilized Section 80C or 80D capacity based on historical spending), the neural tier implements unsupervised behavioral modeling:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{K-Means Clustering:} Categorizes transactions into semantic clusters $\mathcal{C} = \{\text{Living}, \text{Discretionary}, \text{Tax-Deductible}, \text{Debt Service}\}$ based on four features: normalized transaction magnitude, recurrence periodicity, transaction day-of-month, and counterparty Merchant Category Code (MCC) encoding~\cite{macqueen1967kmeans}.
    \item \textbf{DBSCAN Anomaly Detection:} Uncovers irregular transaction spikes and potential duplicate billing without supervision ($\epsilon = 0.5, \text{MinPts} = 3$)~\cite{ester1996dbscan}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Three-Tier Production ML Architecture}
To achieve high throughput and fault tolerance, FinFlow employs a three-tier architecture for ML analytics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (FastAPI REST Microservice):} An asynchronous Python 3 service running on port 8001 utilizing vectorized NumPy and Scikit-Learn pipelines. This microservice delivers batch clustering in $< 15$ ms.
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (CLI Subprocess Fallback):} If the FastAPI HTTP service is unreachable, the Node.js ingestion backend invokes a local Python CLI script passing payload buffers over IPC.
    \item \textbf{Tier 3 (In-Process TypeScript Fallback):} If Python dependencies are absent, an in-process heuristic clustering algorithm runs directly in Node.js, ensuring uninterrupted user experience.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Context-Grounded Virtual CA Protocol}
To provide natural conversational assistance without risking regulatory non-compliance, FinFlow employs a strict context-grounding protocol. Traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)~\cite{lewis2020retrieval} relies on semantic embedding proximity; however, semantic proximity is not equivalent to statutory or arithmetic validity. Asking an LLM to compute slab taxes or evaluate ITR eligibility directly from raw text invariably triggers confabulation.

Instead, the application server compiles the reconciled output of the Multi-Source Core, Form Selector, and Tax Engine into an authoritative, immutable JSON state object:

The LLM's system prompt enforces a formal contract: the model is strictly forbidden from recomputing arithmetic figures or overriding statutory citations. Its inferential authority is confined to semantic synthesis, explaining why the New Regime yields ₹33,800 in net savings, why unlisted equity shares necessitate filing ITR-2 rather than ITR-1, and clarifying how adopting the higher AIS savings interest figure shields the taxpayer from a Section 143(1)(a) demand notice.

\subsection{Algorithmic Tax-Saving Strategy Optimizer and Capital Efficiency Ranking}
\label{sec:tax_optimizer}
Beyond baseline reconciliation and retrospective compliance, a professional Chartered Accountant delivers primary economic value through forward-looking, personalized tax-reduction advisory. To automate this capability deterministically without neural hallucinations, FinFlow formulates tax-saving recommendation as a constrained capital allocation problem.

Let $\mathcal{R} = \{r_1, r_2, \dots, r_m\}$ represent the statutory rule set of deduction and exemption provisions under the Income Tax Act, 1961 (including Sections 80C, 80CCD(1B), 80CCD(2), 80D, 24(b), 80E, 80EEA, 80EEB, 80GG, and 44ADA). Each statutory rule $r_j$ is characterized by a tuple:
\begin{equation}
r_j = \langle C_j^{\max}, \Omega_j, O_j, \tau_j \rangle
\end{equation}
where $C_j^{\max}$ denotes the legal maximum monetary ceiling, $\Omega_j \subseteq \{\text{OLD}, \text{NEW}\}$ denotes regime applicability, $O_j$ represents the marginal out-of-pocket cash outlay required, and $\tau_j$ is the statutory citation.

For a taxpayer profile with income $Y$, current utilized deduction $u_j$, and declared deductions $\mathbf{u}$, the unutilized deduction headroom is:
\begin{equation}
g_j = \max(0, C_j^{\max} - u_j)
\end{equation}
The marginal tax benefit $\Delta T_j$ achievable by claiming headroom $g_j$ is evaluated directly through the deterministic engine:
\begin{equation}
\Delta T_j = T(Y, \mathbf{u}) - T(Y, \mathbf{u} \oplus g_j)
\end{equation}
where $\mu(Y) = \frac{\Delta T_j}{g_j}$ reflects the taxpayer's effective marginal tax bracket inclusive of the 4\% Health and Education Cess.

To rank candidate strategies for individual taxpayers, FinFlow computes a \textit{Capital Efficiency Index} $\rho_j$:
\begin{equation}
\rho_j = \begin{cases}
\infty & \text{if } O_j = 0 \text{ (Zero-outlay restructuring)} \\
\frac{\Delta T_j}{O_j} \times 100\% & \text{if } O_j > 0
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Strategies are sorted lexicographically: $\rho_j = \infty$ zero-outlay salary or business restructurings receive absolute priority, followed by investments sorted descending by net rupee savings $\Delta T_j$.

\textbf{Zero-Outlay Arbitrage Mechanisms:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Employer Corporate NPS (Section 80CCD(2)):} Diverting up to 10\% of Basic salary directly from employer Cost-to-Company (CTC) yields an immediate tax shield without diminishing post-tax savings. Uniquely under Section 115BAC, Section 80CCD(2) is permitted under \textit{both} Old and New Tax Regimes, providing an unconstrained $\rho_j = \infty$ optimization opportunity for salaried employees.
    \item \textbf{Presumptive Professional Taxation (Section 44ADA):} For freelance software engineers, doctors, and consultants with gross receipts $Y_{\text{gross}} \le 7.5\text{M INR}$, Section 44ADA deems exactly 50\% of gross receipts as taxable profit ($Y_{\text{presumptive}} = 0.50 \cdot Y_{\text{gross}}$) with zero book-keeping or audit requirements. The resulting tax reduction $\Delta T_{44ADA} = T(Y_{\text{gross}}) - T(0.50 \cdot Y_{\text{gross}})$ yields an instantaneous tax halving at $O_{44ADA} = 0$.
    \item \textbf{Section 87A Marginal Relief Zone Detection:} For taxpayers with New Regime taxable income falling into the critical threshold zone $700,000 < Y \le 727,777$ INR, the incremental tax is legally restricted to $(Y - 700,000)$. FinFlow identifies micro-adjustments that eliminate tax liability entirely.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Account Aggregator (AA) Horizon}
\label{sec:aa_horizon}
While document-based ingestion (PDFs) remains ubiquitous in retail finance, India's financial data architecture is transitioning toward the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Account Aggregator (AA) ecosystem~\cite{rebit2021accountaggregator}. Under the ReBIT technical standard, Financial Information Users (FIUs) request digitally signed financial artifacts from Financial Information Providers (FIPs) through user-mediated consent handles.

FinFlow's symbolic architecture provides a natural bridge to this paradigm. By decoupling statement ingestion from statutory logic, the parser layer can ingest JSON/XML FI-Fetch payloads alongside raw PDFs. Furthermore, FinFlow's balance continuity invariant ($|B_i - (B_{i-1} + C_i - D_i)| \le 0.01$) serves as a continuous real-time audit invariant, detecting upstream core banking reconciliation discrepancies or dropped asynchronous packets even within regulated open banking data feeds.

\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}
While FinFlow establishes formal guarantees within parsed tabular streams, several practical considerations exist:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{OCR Quality Degradation:} Low-resolution mobile camera scans of physical bank passbooks may degrade OCR text recognition. However, FinFlow's balance continuity invariant guarantees that any dropped digit is caught immediately rather than silently propagated to the tax calculation.
    \item \textbf{Legislative Evolution:} Indian Union Budgets annually amend slab thresholds. Because the statutory engine is modularized into declarative rule files, updating tax parameters requires no neural retraining.
\end{itemize}
